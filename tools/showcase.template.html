One illustration, two surfaces

The Windows illustration library is being rebuilt around a single constraint: no light and dark variants. Every asset ships once and has to hold its silhouette on both Windows surfaces. That one rule decides the palette, the elevation, and which shapes are allowed to exist.

The pilot set

Every tile is split down the middle

Left half sits on the Windows light surface, right half on dark. Same asset, no variant. Where an asset holds together across the seam, it passes.

The delivery ramp

512, 256, 64, at true relative scale

An illustration that only works big is not finished. Shown here at the three delivery sizes in proportion, so the small end is where problems show rather than where they hide.

The ramp found something.

The three gradient marks hold their read all the way down. Most of the layered assets do not: the keyboards, both laptops, device sync and the news card collapse into grey mush at 64. Layered planes need pixels to read as layers. Either 64 is not a real delivery size for that treatment, or those assets need a simplified small variant.

Palette

Only one step per family survives both surfaces

Measured contrast against each Windows surface. A step is usable as the outermost shape only when it clears 3:1 on both, and the dual-safe band turns out to be exactly one step wide.

TokenHexOn lightOn darkOutermost
The step names do not mean the same thing across families.

Blue and purple are dual-safe at loud. Teal is not: teal.loud misses on light by 0.11, and teal's safe step is heavy instead. A designer writing teal.loud expecting it to behave like blue.loud gets an asset that disappears on light surfaces. #359b91, two percent darker, clears at 3.03 and puts the family back in step.

Elevation

Six recipes, distilled from forty-one values

The Figma file carried 41 distinct effect values across roughly 100 applications. Reading them showed the variation was opacity drift rather than intent, so they collapse to named recipes an asset can only pick from.

Parts

Shapes are geometry, so they are generated

Base shapes come out of a generator rather than being drawn, which is what lets size be a property of a layer instead of a different shape. Three sizes fitted by bounding box, so a triangle and a hexagon of the same size look the same size, with the corner radius held constant because that was already the convention in the file.

Base shapes

Each at lg 64, md 48, sm 32, being 100%, 75% and 50% of the 64 safe area, all on the 8 grid.

Secondary shapes

Movable and scalable, and constrained to sit inside their base. That containment is checked by rasterising, because a bounding box says a shield fits a triangle when its corners plainly do not.

What comes next

Scaling the library without loosening the rules

Generation

The pilot set is packaged as a training corpus: 16 assets at 512 with captions written from the system rather than typed, split into the two treatments the set actually contains so a model can be asked for either. Untrained as of today.

The check stays

Whatever generates an asset, it still has to clear 3:1 on both surfaces and hold its read at 64. The measurements on this page are the bar, and they apply to generated output exactly as they apply to drawn output.

The builder

A composition tool already reads the same tokens and parts, renders to SVG, and runs the surfaces check live while you work. Recipes are data, so an asset can be diffed, reviewed and rebuilt rather than redrawn.
